## Why this document exists

The website publishes **starting prices**, service descriptions and broad deliverables. The scope below converts those into **bounded starter packages** so Reboot Syndicate can identify additional work, protect time and profit, and explain expectations clearly. Numbers (pages, posts, hours, revisions, etc.) are **recommended starting limits**, not proven terms of a client's existing contract. Approve and customize them before quoting; provide the agreed scope in writing.

## Core operating rules

1. **The signed Statement of Work (SOW) controls.** A website starting price alone is not a promise of unlimited work. Never change an existing client's terms retroactively.
2. **Scope by measurable units:** pages, products, URLs optimized, posts, campaigns, platforms, locations, automations, events, revision rounds, support time and deadlines.
3. **One revision round** means one consolidated list of changes to the existing approved direction, not a new design or new project requirement. The client sends all feedback for a round at once.
4. **Client supplies/accesses:** business details, credentials through secure approved sharing, copy/images unless quoted, legal policies, permissions and timely approvals. Never request passwords in email or place credentials in source control.
5. **Third-party costs separate:** ad budgets, domain renewals, web/email/SMS hosting, software subscriptions, payment processing, licensed assets, postage, hardware, and relevant taxes unless explicitly itemized as included.
6. **No performance guarantees:** no guaranteed search ranking, ad return, leads, account verification, review deletion, sales, email deliverability or data recovery.
7. **Ownership and handoff:** define who owns the domain, hosting, ad/analytics accounts, final assets, source files, and ongoing access. Keep clients in control of their core accounts.
8. **Scope changes require written approval before work:** describe change, fee, deadline impact and payment trigger. Log the approval with the project.
9. **Do not silently donate additional work.** Small goodwill accommodations are optional owner decisions and should be recorded, not assumed.
10. **Safety/compliance:** permission-based customer outreach, ethical review practices, privacy/consent controls and platform rules are mandatory. Specific legal obligations should be independently reviewed.

## Quick decision rule

- **Included:** specifically named in the signed deliverables AND within the approved quantity/complexity/revision limit.
- **Clarification:** explains or corrects an included deliverable without changing the quantity or fundamental direction.
- **Change order:** adds a feature, increases the quantity, changes approved direction, adds a platform/location/integration, expands the deadline burden or exceeds a revision/time cap.
- **New project:** materially different business objective or separate service line; quote independently.

## How to quote and approve changes

When a customer requests something new, use this internal checklist **before doing the extra work**:

1. Record the request in writing and reference the current SOW/version.
2. Compare it with the approved units, assets, platform count, revision rounds, support hours and exclusions.
3. Estimate incremental hours, cash costs, complexity and risk. Quote a fixed add-on fee or capped hourly budget; never guess that the starting price covers it.
4. Explain the deliverable and any change in delivery date, acceptance conditions and third-party fees.
5. Obtain written approval and any agreed additional payment before beginning.
6. Update the change-order log and project deliverables list.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| YYYY-MM-DD | Example HVAC site | Add appointment booking | Initial quote includes contact form only | $___ | +__ business days | Pending | Not started |

### Example change-order message

> Happy to add that. The current proposal includes **[agreed deliverable/quantity]**. **[New request]** is outside that scope, so I can add it for **$[fee]**, with **[schedule change]**. If you approve this adjustment in writing, I'll update the work order and proceed.

## Internal preflight before accepting a project

**Qualification:** Does the business actually need this service? What outcomes are being sought (without guarantees)?  
**Feasibility:** Platforms, integrations, assets, credentials, existing website conditions, unknowns and third-party fees checked?  
**Economics:** What is the target gross margin? What is the maximum labor budget at this quoted price?  
**Delivery:** Who supplies content, approves drafts, handles hosting/accounts, and signs off on completion?  
**Controls:** Revisions, extra pages/products/platforms, milestones, payment schedule, cancellation, intellectual property and support after handoff documented?  
**Approvals:** A client-facing SOW with inclusions, exclusions, assumptions and change-order rules signed/accepted?

## Suggested acceptance and aftercare terms (approve before use)

- Completion means the stated deliverables function according to agreed acceptance criteria, not that revenue/traffic/rankings improve.
- Client gets one consolidated acceptance review within a **proposed five business days** of delivery. This is a suggested term, not current policy.
- Bugs caused by the delivered agreed work should be corrected under a clearly defined **proposed 14-day workmanship window**, limited to reproducible defects. New requests, third-party failures and content changes are not defects.
- Ongoing maintenance, platform subscriptions and new features require a separate maintenance agreement or change order.
- A local attorney should review formal contract terms, liability limits, data handling and any payment/cancellation clauses before use as a binding agreement.
